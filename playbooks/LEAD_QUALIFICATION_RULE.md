# Lead Qualification Rule (v12) — Fit × Offer-Acquisition Intent

What: the single rule of record for deciding whether any item — a post, comment, caption, or people-row — is a lead, and what kind. It replaces separate ad hoc lead heuristics with one general test, industry-independent, run against each client's own `buyer_profile`.

Who loads it: Stage 10 Detection Workflow (`playbooks/10_LEAD_COMPETITOR_DETECTION.md`); the Social Discovery Pass (feed/search, people-search rows on Facebook, Instagram, X); the lead-engine skill's extractor tier (`playbooks/skills/lead-engine/`); Step 5 below, which records a post as a `discovered` source in the client's source registry (`tool source-registry`, kind `discovered`) for the Boss to approve — nothing is harvested automatically; the harvest job that runs only on the Boss's explicit order (`playbooks/COMMENT_TRIAGE_RULE.md`, a separate rule applied to the thread's individual authors once a discovered source is approved); and the regression test (`playbooks/tests/lead-rule/`).

Edit policy: any change to this file requires re-running `playbooks/tests/lead-rule` per its `RUN.md` and attaching the resulting report before the change is considered done — do not edit this rule and move on without that report.

The text below is the owner-approved v12 rule. It is deliberately domain-agnostic: replace `sells`, `types` and `why_they_need` with the current client's facts, but do not change the evidence standard by industry.

---

Qualify ONE item (a post, comment, caption, or profile row) for ONE client. Decide WHO the person is, apply the competitor/noise relationship override, then evaluate WHAT problem is shown, HOW they are resolving it or acquiring outside capability, and WHEN action is needed before reading the decision off the matrix. Keep those dimensions independent. Reason from the item's own language, never magic phrases; never require a stated need to decide who someone is.

## Inputs

- CLIENT buyer profile: `sells`, `sells_to` (who buys, as membership types and situations), `types` (the same memberships, one per line — pick the closest), `why_they_need`, `location` (if it matters), `competitors`, `not_buyers`. An activity the product helps with (making video, buying insurance, marketing) is never a condition of membership.
- ITEM: platform, surface, the community it was found in, the author line (subtitle, bio or headline, if any), the text, a date note.

## Step 1 — WHO is this person? (fit)

First name the person: `person_type` = what they are, in a few words (trade, role, business or situation), inferred from every clue — what they do, own or run, where they are, their stage of life, the community, handle, hashtags, language. Infer the ordinary case: a listing post is a real-estate agent; quoting rates is a loan officer or insurance agent (self-employed, own pipeline, even if lender-affiliated); salon work is a salon owner; app revenue is a founder. A call to action aimed at THEIR OWN customers ("DM me for a quote") is evidence of what they do, never their own need.

Then run the MEMBERSHIP TEST: go down THIS client's own `types` (never another client's) and pick the closest line as `sells_to_match` (or "none"), asking only "is this person one of those?" — answered from who they are. The CATEGORY before the colon or before "e.g." is the test; the names after are examples, never a closed list — "any trade" also matches a dentist, an escrow officer, a videographer. "No evidence of doing X" (making video, marketing, buying coverage) is forbidden as a reason. Selling a service to others (coaching, an agency, freelancing) does not itself lower fit or make them a competitor — Step 2 decides that.

- `fit = high`: `sells_to_match` is a real match, and, when `location` matters, the person is inside the service area or arriving into it (a stated move there, a job there, a date) — arriving counts as inside for LOCATION.
- `fit = medium`: an adjacent profession or situation; the right kind of person whose location is unclear when location matters (an exact match with no location stays medium); a situation already settled with nothing ahead for a service tied to one transaction ("just bought") — except where `types` says a settled case still counts; or a real change with no named transaction where `types` requires one (arriving alone isn't a named purchase or lease).
- `fit = low`: no match in `types`, evidence of being outside `sells_to`; a plan hedged as possibly not happening ("maybe X or maybe not") counts here, not as medium; or a different specific situation than the one described (refinancing is not a home purchase).

Write the membership conclusion in `fit_reason` and the concrete item facts that support it in `fit_evidence`; do not use an inferred need as fit evidence.

Community context: the author's OWN words decide their profession — strip the group name and ask whether the TEXT ALONE shows what THEY DO for others, versus only something that happened to THEM personally; a personal post ("I'm stuck with") inside a professional-sounding group does not by itself make the author that profession — treat as unknown/consumer unless the text shows the trade. For LOCATION, a community whose name states a place ("Orange County", "California") is evidence of place; a trade or national name ("Realtors USA", "Việt") is not. A bio-only profile row is judged on its own claim; a missing post never lowers fit. Uncertainty about timing lowers fit to medium; uncertainty about employment structure or exact trade within an "ANY" line does not.

## Step 2 — Competitor, partner or noise? (decide this BEFORE intent)

1. `competitor`: sells the same thing as `sells`, to the same people and market — another agent, broker, officer, agency, app, tool or provider (same trade outside `location`/`not_buyers` is not a competitor). Ask: "whose problem does their work solve, their own or a paying customer's?" A freelancer or agency selling THIS service to others is a competitor; doing the same work only for their own business is not. This runs before `not_buyers` and before the matrix: a same-market seller is `competitor` even with a shown need — unless `sells_to`/`types` names that profession as a customer or partner, then treat as a customer.
2. `none` (noise): the person cannot buy this offer in any reading — a job seeker, a student, spam or multi-level selling, a meme or political post, the client themselves; or the text negates the need in any language ("not looking", "already have this", "đừng pitch nữa") — not merely solving a related problem another way; or the item is stale when timing matters. A high-fit person who explicitly rejects this kind of offer is `none`, not `warm`.
3. Referral partner: sends buyers rather than buying (an agent for a lender, a lender for an insurer) — a lead only when `types` names that role; `none` when `not_buyers` names it; `watch` when neither does. Check only this client's own roster, never another's.

Record this axis separately as `relationship_type = buyer|partner|competitor|none`: a matched potential customer is `buyer`; a referral role named or retained as a relationship is `partner`; the same-market seller in rule 1 is `competitor`; and noise in rule 2 is `none`. Relationship does not substitute for lead temperature.

## Step 3 — WHY NOW? (offer-acquisition intent)

Intent is observable evidence that THIS PERSON or their organization is trying to obtain `sells` as an externally supplied offer or capability now or soon. Here, external offer/capability is domain-neutral: it can be a product, service, provider, tool, coverage, representation, transaction, partner or other thing the client actually sells. It is not the mere existence of a problem that `sells` could solve. Evaluate the following dimensions separately before assigning intent:

- `problem_outcome`: name the exact friction or outcome in this client's `why_they_need`, or `none` when the item shows none.
- `problem_relevance = proven|plausible|none`: `proven` means the item shows that exact friction or outcome; `plausible` means the person's situation could create it, but the item does not show that it has; `none` means the shown objective is unrelated or contradictory. Put the item's facts, including whose problem it is, in `problem_evidence`.
- `problem_state = unresolved|resolved|unknown`: `unresolved` requires evidence that the relevant gap remains open; `resolved` means it has already been handled; `unknown` means the item does not say. Put the evidence in `unresolved_evidence`, stating `none` when absent.
- `resolution_activity = active|passive|none`: `active` means the person is presently taking steps to solve the exact relevant problem; `passive` means they only describe, notice, discuss or contemplate it; `none` means no resolution action is shown. Asking peers for experience or applying a self-directed tactic may be `active`, but activity alone is not buying intent. Put the observed action in `active_resolution_evidence`, stating `none` when absent.
- `acquisition_posture = explicit|open|none`: `explicit` means they directly seek, compare, evaluate, request or complain about this exact kind of external offer or provider. `open` means observable facts show willingness or necessity to bring in external capability — for example a failed internal attempt together with a stated capability/resource gap, an approved budget, an external shortlist, or a committed outsourcing decision — without a direct provider request. `none` means no such posture is shown. Put only observed facts in `acquisition_evidence`; never manufacture openness from a likely benefit.
- `urgency = immediate|soon|not_shown`: `immediate` means a present failure or committed near deadline demands action now; `soon` means a bounded upcoming event makes action time-sensitive; `not_shown` means neither is evidenced. Put the date, deadline or present consequence in `timing_evidence`, stating `none` when absent. Urgency is independent of intent.

**Declared objective controls; no domain transfer.** When the item says what the person is trying to obtain, solve or hire for, treat that as the objective. A request for a technical cofounder does not become demand for marketing; a request for bookkeeping does not become demand for insurance; a move does not become demand for a mortgage unless the item independently shows an acquisition posture toward financing. A broad event such as a launch, move, hire, birth, breakdown, deadline or growth milestone can make another need plausible, but cannot transfer intent into an adjacent domain. If one item contains multiple objectives, each client gets only the evidence independently tied to that client's `sells`.

Before assigning either explicit or implied intent, apply the neutral-reader counterfactual: **if the client's offer and desired classification were hidden, would a neutral reader still conclude that the person is trying or demonstrably prepared to obtain an external capability of this exact kind, rather than merely being someone who could benefit from it?** Record `counterfactual_result = pass` only for a clear yes, `fail` when the evidence instead shows self-resolution, peer learning, self-promotion, ordinary work or another declared objective, and `not_shown` when the item is insufficient to decide. Only `pass` may produce explicit or implied intent.

Assign intent only after those dimensions:

- `intent = explicit`: ownership is shown, `problem_relevance = proven`, `problem_state = unresolved`, `acquisition_posture = explicit`, and the counterfactual passes. The direct acquisition action itself counts as `resolution_activity = active`.
- `intent = implied`: all of the following are evidenced: (1) the problem belongs to this person or their organization; (2) `problem_relevance = proven`, with exact problem/outcome alignment to `sells`; (3) `problem_state = unresolved`; (4) `resolution_activity = active`; (5) `acquisition_posture = open`; and (6) the counterfactual passes.
- `intent = none`: every other case, including missing evidence for any required implied-intent condition.

Write the final gate result in `intent_reason`, citing the canonical evidence fields rather than adding a new evidence key.

Self-directed action, peer learning, self-promotion, a call to action aimed at one's own customers, ordinary professional work, a biography, a launch announcement, a milestone, and a situation that merely “could benefit” from `sells` never establish buying intent. They may prove fit, problem relevance or active resolution, but with `acquisition_posture = none` the intent remains `none`. For a partner segment, intent requires acquiring, comparing or addressing a problem with that kind of partner; the partner's normal work remains `none`.

A person can be a perfect customer, have a real unresolved problem and even be actively working on it, yet have `intent = none` because no acquisition posture is observable. That is normal and still valuable: high fit plus no intent becomes `warm`.

## Step 4 — Decision (the matrix, the same for every industry)

| fit | intent explicit | intent implied | intent none |
|---|---|---|---|
| high | **hot** | **hot** | **warm** |
| medium | **warm** | **watch** | none |
| low | **watch** | none | none |

Compute fit and intent independently, then write the matrix result as `lead_level = hot|warm|watch|none` — `watch` is not a catch-all for "uncertain". `competitor`/`none` from Step 2 override the matrix and force `lead_level = none`; preserve the compatibility field `decision = competitor` for a competitor and otherwise set `decision` to `lead_level`. `urgency` ranks records only within the resulting `lead_level`; it never changes `intent` and never promotes a record to `hot`.

**Hot evidence completeness gate.** Every `lead_level = hot` row must contain non-placeholder `fit_evidence`, `problem_outcome`, `problem_evidence`, `unresolved_evidence`, `active_resolution_evidence`, `acquisition_evidence`, and `confidence`. `timing_evidence` is required only when timing is actually shown; `urgency = not_shown` is valid for Hot. Each field must identify the fact that supports its own dimension. Do not paste one generic evidence sentence into fit, problem, intent and urgency; if one source sentence contains several distinct facts, cite only the relevant clause or fact in each field. Missing required Hot evidence downgrades the row to the matrix result for `intent = none` rather than dropping the lead.

## Examples (examples, not a list)

These cases illustrate the evidence structure; their words are not trigger phrases and must never be used as a keyword list.

1. An insurance agent posts a new-business announcement and invites customers to request a quote. For a service that sells customer acquisition to independent professionals: fit high, self-promotion, `acquisition_posture = none`, intent none → warm. For another insurance agency in the same market: competitor.
2. A restaurant owner announces an opening in three weeks and promotes reservations. For a local marketing firm: the opening may make customer acquisition plausible and urgency soon, but promotion is self-directed and shows no external acquisition posture → warm when fit is high. If the owner instead says reservations remain empty, their attempted campaigns failed, nobody on the team can run acquisition, and budget has been approved for outside capability, the exact problem is unresolved, resolution is active, posture is open and the neutral-reader test may support implied intent → hot when fit is high.
3. A factory manager reports a coming safety-audit deadline. For a safety consultancy: deadline alone shows urgency, not buying intent. If the manager also says the internal team lacks the required expertise and external-support budget is approved, the exact unresolved problem plus active/open acquisition posture may be implied → hot when fit is high. If the post asks firms for proposals, intent is explicit.
4. A new parent announces a baby's birth. For a life-insurance adviser: a new dependent can make the need plausible, but without an unresolved coverage gap or acquisition posture, intent is none → warm when fit is high. If the parent says current coverage is insufficient and asks to compare advisers or policies, intent is explicit → hot when fit is high.
5. A software founder launching a marketplace says they are seeking a CTO-level partner. For a sales-and-marketing provider: the declared objective is technical; the launch cannot transfer that intent into marketing → warm when founder fit is high. For a provider whose exact offer is CTO search or technical cofounder matching, the same evidence can be explicit intent.
6. A shop owner asks peers how they repaired a failed refrigeration unit and says they are trying a do-it-yourself fix. For a commercial repair company: the equipment problem is relevant and resolution may be active, but peer learning and self-repair show no acquisition posture → warm when fit is high. Asking for a qualified technician available today would instead be explicit intent; `immediate` urgency would rank it above other hot leads, not create the hot tier.
7. A homeowner says they are moving next month but does not say whether they will buy, rent or sell. For a real-estate buyer's agent, the move may be plausible but does not show an exact unresolved purchase problem or an acquisition posture → intent none. If they state they have decided to buy, cannot navigate the local market and have begun evaluating outside representation, those independent facts may establish implied intent; a direct request for agent recommendations is explicit.
8. A profile says only "Licensed insurance agent, bilingual, Garden Grove." For a video service whose buyer profile includes independent agents: fit high, intent none → warm. For an unrelated consumer lender whose `types` do not include that role: fit low → none.

## Step 5 — Is this post a lead source in its comments? (comment_source)

Independently of Steps 1–4, decide whether the people who ANSWER this post will be the client's buyers. Walk these questions in order, record each answer in `comment_source_reason`, and stop at the first question that settles it.

1. **Does the post ASK for replies from others?** Yes: a request for a provider, a recommendation, a referral, a quote, an expert answer; a question only one trade can answer; a peer question addressed to one audience ("Coaches — how do you…", "Fellow agents…"); a gathering question ("who else is moving to Irvine this summer?", "first-time buyers, what surprised you?"). No: a statement or a PROMOTION — a listing, a showcase, an update, a rate note, a launch, a milestone, market commentary, a complaint with no question, a recruiting ad, and any call to action that offers the author's OWN service ("anyone need an editor, DM me", "doors open in January", "ai cần pre-approval nhắn mình", "tag someone who is looking"). The author of a promotion is selling, not asking; the people who answer a promotion are that author's customers, not a gathering the client can harvest. Statement or promotion → `unlikely`, stop.
2. **Name the answerer type.** Who will reply — a trade ("realtors", "loan officers", "personal trainers", "tutors", "plumbers") or a situation ("first-time buyers in Orange County", "new parents in Irvine", "Tesla owners in California", "movers into Irvine")? Use the post's own words. A request for a provider is answered by providers of that trade; a peer or gathering question is answered by that audience; a question about a purchase, a policy, a loan is answered by peers in that situation AND by providers of that trade — name both.
3. **Membership test — write `types_match`.** Go down the client's `types` and copy the line that the answerer type belongs to, verbatim (or "none"). Rules for matching:
   - Answerers who are PROVIDERS of any trade (people who sell a service or run a business, whatever the trade) match any line whose category word is "independent professional of ANY trade", "self-employed professional of ANY trade", "owner of ANY small business", "creator, course seller, freelancer of ANY kind" — regardless of the client's own product. A loan officer, a personal trainer, a tutor, a plumber, a videographer answering a request are self-employed professionals; they match such a line for a marketing service, a video app AND a small-business insurer alike.
   - Answerers in a SITUATION match a line that names that situation (buyers, movers, new parents, a new asset, a rising premium, a new address, holders of a mortgage).
   - "none" → `unlikely`, stop.
4. **Own-trade check (runs even after a match).** If the answerer type sells what the client sells — realtors for a realtor client, lenders for a lender client, insurance agents for an insurance client, video editors for a video-editing app — they are competitors → `unlikely`, unless `types` names that trade as a partner or referral segment, then `likely`. When question 2 named both peers and providers: a post that ASKS FOR a provider ("ai biết agent bảo hiểm nào…", "recommend a lender") is answered by that provider — apply this check to the provider; a peer or gathering question with no provider ask ("có ai bị vậy không?", "who else is refinancing?") is answered by the peers — apply it to the peers.
5. **Location.** When `location` matters and the post places the answerers outside the client's area with no move into it → `unlikely`. A post whose place is unknown or inside the area → `likely`.

`comment_source` is a property of the THREAD, not of the author: a consumer's post can be `none` for its author and `likely` as a source. Nothing is harvested automatically from a `likely` post; it is only recorded for the Boss to approve.

Examples (examples, not a list): "Cần tìm realtor để listing nhà ở Westminster" — Q1 request; Q2 realtors; Q3 `types_match` = the "ANY trade" line for a marketing service, a video app, a small-business insurer in that state, and the partner line for a lender; Q4 for a realtor client → competitors → unlikely. "Anyone have a personal trainer in Irvine?" — Q2 trainers → the same ANY-trade clients → likely; a realtor or a lender → no match → unlikely. "Premium bảo hiểm xe tăng gấp đôi, có ai bị vậy không?" (no provider asked) — Q2 peers with a rising premium; for an insurer: they match "rising premium" → likely; for a marketing service: peers are consumers → no match → unlikely. "Ai biết agent bảo hiểm nhà tốt giới thiệu dùm?" (a provider asked) — Q2 insurance agents; for an insurer: own trade → unlikely; for a marketing service or a video app: agents match ANY trade → likely. "Who else is moving to Irvine this summer?" — Q2 movers → likely for a realtor, a lender and an insurer that names a new address; unlikely for clients that sell to professionals. "Just listed 4bd in Irvine, open house Saturday" and "Anyone need a fast editor? DM me" — Q1 promotion → unlikely for everyone.

Output adds three fields: `"types_match": "verbatim line or none", "comment_source": "likely|unlikely", "comment_source_reason": "Q1 … Q2 … Q3 … Q4 … Q5 …"`.
